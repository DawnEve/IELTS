<b>SAS’s exit follows a growing list of American technology firms scaling back operations in China amid rising competition and geopolitical strain.</b>

After more than two decades in China, American software giant SAS Institute has officially withdrawn from the market, marking the end of a long-standing presence in the country. The analytics and AI specialist has reportedly laid off around 400 employees as part of its exit, according to the South China Morning Post. The layoffs were announced via email and a brief video call, during which executives cited “organisational optimisation” as the reason for the decision. Employees were asked to sign separation agreements by November 14.

The compensation package reportedly includes one month’s salary for every year of service, plus two additional months of pay, an annual bonus, and salary through the end of 2025. SAS is expected to issue an official statement next week, though its simplified Chinese website and local job listings have already gone offline.

In a statement to SCMP, a SAS spokesperson confirmed: “SAS is ceasing direct business operations in China. This decision reflects a broader shift in how we operate globally, optimising our footprint and ensuring long-term sustainability.” However, SAS will continue to have a limited presence through third-party partners.

Founded in 1976 and headquartered in Cary, North Carolina, SAS entered China in 1999, later opening an R&D and support centre in Beijing in 2005. The company was consistently recognised as one of China’s best employers, earning the Top Employer China title for 17 consecutive years from the Netherlands-based Top Employers Institute.
